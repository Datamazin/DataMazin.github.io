Bringing Simplicity to Monthly Financial Reporting

Before I built Power BI models for a living, I built the month-end reports myself — the exports, the reconciliations, the workbook that only one person truly understood. That background is why I approach finance dashboards differently: the goal isn't a prettier report, it's a close that doesn't depend on heroics.

Most finance teams don't have a data problem. They have a plumbing problem. The numbers are correct; getting to them takes four days and two people who can't take vacation in the same week.

Five things that simplify the monthly close

  • Refresh on a schedule, not by hand. When numbers pull straight from the ERP or accounting system, the close starts with analysis instead of exports.
  • Consolidate systems once, in the model. SQL, Point 0f Service, a payroll file, a bank feed — the reconciliation logic belongs in the semantic model, built once, not rebuilt every month.
  • Remove the single point of failure. A documented, governed model means the close survives turnover, PTO, and "Final_Final_v3.xlsx."
  • Lead with the KPIs that drive decisions. Revenue and gross profit, cash flow, and budget vs. actual variance across the top; trends and variance in the middle; transaction detail built in Paginated Reports for the people who need to drill in.
  • Design by subtraction. Every metric that doesn't change a decision is noise. Cutting them is what turns a report people tolerate into one they actually open.

What it looks like in practice

The shift is subtle but total: the first question in the monthly meeting stops being "is this current?" and becomes "what do we do about it?" In my experience, teams get to light bulb momments in a couple of weeks — not a couple of quarters — because the hard part was never the visuals. It was the messy data maze underneath them.
